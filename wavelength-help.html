📡 How to Play Wavelength

One person can see a secret spot on a dial. Everyone else can't. Their only hope is a clue made of words — and words are a surprisingly leaky way to describe a point in space.

The dial, in one breath

The dial is a spectrum between two opposite ideas — say, Cold on one end and Hot on the other. Somewhere along that line sits a hidden target. The Psychic can see exactly where it is. Nobody else can.

The Psychic gives a clue — a word, a phrase, whatever helps — meant to describe roughly where that hidden spot falls. Everyone else listens, then drags a needle to where they think it is. The closer you land, the more points you score.

Scoring zones

4 points Dead on. This zone is deliberately tiny — a real bullseye should be hard.
3 points Close. A solid, satisfying guess.
2 points Nearby. You were listening, at least.
1 point Everywhere else. Every guess earns something — nobody walks away with zero.

The zones only appear once a guess has actually been locked in — nobody sees them early enough to cheat off them.

Two ways to play

👥 Group Mode

One Psychic stays in that role for the entire game and never scores points themselves. Everyone else is guessing every round, all chasing the same target score — first to reach it wins. Great for 3 or more people.

⚔️ Head to Head

Exactly two players (or two teams). The Psychic and Guesser roles swap every round — so everyone takes turns doing both jobs, and both sides build up a real score. First to the target wins. This is the one built for exactly two people.

Online vs. Pass & Play

The rules are identical either way — only the mechanics of hiding information differ.

📶 Online

Everyone's on their own device. The Psychic's target and each guess stay private to your own screen until the moment they should be revealed — nothing to hide physically, the app handles it.

🤝 Pass & Play

One shared device. The Psychic taps to privately view their target, then hides it before passing the phone on. Guessers go one at a time, each locking in their guess before handing it to the next person — so nobody's guess can be influenced by seeing someone else's needle first.

A round, start to finish

  1. Pick a spectrum. Random from a built-in list, or make one up on the spot — "Guilty Pleasure ↔ Actually Terrible" works just as well as anything official.
  2. The Psychic reveals their target — privately — and studies it.
  3. The clue. Out loud, in your own words. A phrase is fair game, not just one word.
  4. Guessers place the needle based on nothing but that clue.
  5. The reveal. Zones appear, scores tally up, and everyone finds out how well the clue actually worked.

The best clues aren't the safest ones. Say what you actually think.
